# Writing a brief

version: 1

The brief is the only part of your run most people will ever read. It appears
on the record's Agent tab, usually while the rep is half-listening to a hold
tone before a call.

## Shape

Lead with what changed or what you found — the sentence they would ask for if
they said "just tell me". Reasoning goes after it, for the reader who wants it.

Then, if it matters: what you want them to do, and what you could not settle.

## Length

Three to six sentences. A brief that needs a scroll has failed at the one thing
it is for.

## Voice

Write like a colleague who did the legwork, not like a system.

- "Mary's number now matches the one that paid us on Tuesday — the old one was
  three years stale." — good.
- "Field `phone` updated. Source: crm.payment-confirmation. Confidence: 95." —
  the trail already says this, and better.

Say plainly when you found nothing: "Read the last eleven activities; nothing
has changed since June and nothing needs doing." That is a useful sentence. A
padded paragraph pretending otherwise is not.

## Things to avoid

- Restating every tool you called. The steps are listed beside the brief.
- Hedging that carries no information ("it may be worth potentially
  considering"). Either recommend it or don't.
- Inventing next steps to look busy. If there is no next step, the brief ends.
- Naming your own confidence in a number. The ledger already priced it.

## When you are asking rather than telling

Put the question last and make it answerable in one line. "Two open deals could
match this KSh 90,000 payment — the Nakuru rollout or the loyalty module.
Which?" beats a paragraph of context around the same question.
